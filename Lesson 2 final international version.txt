<!-- =====================================================
     03. LESSON CONTEXT
====================================================== -->

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        1. Lesson Context
    </h2>

    <p>

        A food chain provides a simplified view of feeding
        relationships. Real ecosystems are more complex:
        organisms usually interact with several other species
        rather than following a single feeding pathway.

    </p>

    <p>

        A food web represents these interconnected
        relationships and helps students understand
        ecological interdependence.

    </p>


    <div class="essential-question">

        Essential Question:<br>

        How can a change in one population affect
        other parts of an ecosystem?

    </div>

</section>

<!-- =====================================================
     06. METHODOLOGY
====================================================== -->

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        4. Teaching and Learning Methodology
    </h2>

    <p>

        The lesson moves from a familiar concept — the food
        chain — toward systems thinking. Students construct
        models, analyse ecological scenarios and justify
        predictions.

    </p>

    <ul>

        <li>
            <strong>Inquiry-based learning:</strong>
            students investigate relationships between organisms.
        </li>

        <li>
            <strong>Systems thinking:</strong>
            students consider how changes can spread through
            interconnected systems.
        </li>

        <li>
            <strong>Collaborative learning:</strong>
            students construct and analyse food webs in groups.
        </li>

        <li>
            <strong>Problem-based learning:</strong>
            students respond to realistic ecological scenarios.
        </li>

        <li>
            <strong>Scientific argumentation:</strong>
            students use evidence to support predictions.
        </li>

        <li>
            <strong>Formative assessment:</strong>
            the teacher monitors understanding throughout
            the lesson.
        </li>

    </ul>

</section>


<!-- =====================================================
     07. FOOD CHAIN VS FOOD WEB
====================================================== -->

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        5. Food Chain vs. Food Web
    </h2>

    <table>

        <thead>

            <tr>

                <th>
                    Food Chain
                </th>

                <th>
                    Food Web
                </th>

            </tr>

        </thead>

        <tbody>

            <tr>

                <td>
                    Represents one feeding pathway.
                </td>

                <td>
                    Represents multiple interconnected
                    feeding pathways.
                </td>

            </tr>

            <tr>

                <td>
                    Usually represented as a linear sequence.
                </td>

                <td>
                    Represents the complexity of ecological
                    relationships.
                </td>

            </tr>

            <tr>

                <td>
                    Useful for introducing energy transfer.
                </td>

                <td>
                    Useful for analysing ecosystem
                    interdependence.
                </td>

            </tr>

            <tr>

                <td>
                    Simpler model.
                </td>

                <td>
                    More realistic representation of
                    many ecosystems.
                </td>

            </tr>

        </tbody>

    </table>

</section>

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        8. Collaborative Investigation
    </h2>

    <p>

        Divide approximately 30 students into six groups
        of about five students.

    </p>


    <h3>
        Ecosystem Investigation
    </h3>

    <p>

        Each group receives a set of organisms representing
        a local or familiar ecosystem.

    </p>


    <h3>
        Suggested Organisms
    </h3>

    <div class="example">

        Producers:
        algae, aquatic plants

        <br><br>

        Primary consumers:
        zooplankton, insects

        <br><br>

        Secondary consumers:
        frogs, small fish

        <br><br>

        Higher-level consumers:
        large fish, herons

    </div>


    <h3>
        Group Task
    </h3>

    <ol>

        <li>
            Identify the producers.
        </li>

        <li>
            Identify the consumers.
        </li>

        <li>
            Determine feeding relationships.
        </li>

        <li>
            Draw arrows showing energy transfer.
        </li>

        <li>
            Construct the food web.
        </li>

        <li>
            Identify trophic levels.
        </li>

        <li>
            Explain why the organisms are interconnected.
        </li>

    </ol>

</section>


<!-- =====================================================
     11. POPULATION CHANGE
====================================================== -->

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        9. Population Change Challenge
    </h2>

    <div class="challenge">

        <strong>
            Ecological Scenario:
        </strong>

        <p>

            Imagine that the population of small fish
            decreases significantly.

        </p>

        <p>

            What might happen to other organisms in
            the food web?

        </p>

    </div>


    <h3>
        Scientific Reasoning Process
    </h3>

    <div class="analysis-flow">

        <div class="analysis-step">

            <strong>1. Predict</strong>

            What might change?

        </div>


        <div class="analysis-step">

            <strong>2. Explain</strong>

            What relationship causes the change?

        </div>


        <div class="analysis-step">

            <strong>3. Trace</strong>

            What other organisms could be affected?

        </div>


        <div class="analysis-step">

            <strong>4. Justify</strong>

            What evidence supports the prediction?

        </div>

    </div>


    <h3>
        Possible Effects
    </h3>

    <ul>

        <li>

            Predators that depend on small fish may have
            less food available.

        </li>

        <li>

            Organisms eaten by small fish may experience
            reduced predation.

        </li>

        <li>

            Population changes may spread through
            interconnected feeding relationships.

        </li>

    </ul>


    <div class="key-message">

        <strong>
            Systems Thinking:
        </strong>

        <p>

            A change in one population does not necessarily
            remain isolated. Because organisms are interconnected,
            ecological effects can move through several
            relationships.

        </p>

    </div>

</section>

<!-- =====================================================
     13. SYSTEMS THINKING
====================================================== -->

<section class="section">

    <h2>
        11. Systems Thinking
    </h2>

    <p>

        Food webs encourage students to view ecosystems
        as interconnected systems rather than collections
        of independent organisms.

    </p>

    <div class="example">

        <strong>
            Key Principle:
        </strong>

        <p>

            Changes in one population can create a sequence
            of direct and indirect effects across an ecosystem.

        </p>

    </div>

    <h3>
        Direct Effect
    </h3>

    <p>

        An effect involving a direct interaction between
        two organisms.

    </p>


    <h3>
        Indirect Effect
    </h3>

    <p>

        An effect that occurs through one or more additional
        ecological relationships.

    </p>

</section>
