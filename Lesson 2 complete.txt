        How can a change in one population affect
        an entire ecosystem?

        Students move from analysing one feeding pathway
        to understanding multiple interconnected feeding
        relationships.

    </p>

<!-- =========================================================
     STEP 10 — POPULATION CHANGE
     ========================================================= -->

<section class="lesson">

    <h2>Step 8 — Population Change Challenge</h2>

    <div class="challenge">

        <strong>Scenario:</strong>

        <p>
            The population of small fish decreases significantly.
        </p>

    </div>

    <h3>Student Reasoning Process</h3>

    <div class="example">

        Predict → Explain → Justify

    </div>

    <h3>Possible Direct Effects</h3>

    <ul>

        <li>
            Large fish have less food.
        </li>

        <li>
            Predators dependent on small fish may experience
            reduced food availability.
        </li>

    </ul>

    <h3>Possible Indirect Effects</h3>

    <ul>

        <li>
            Organisms eaten by small fish may increase.
        </li>

        <li>
            Predator populations may decrease.
        </li>

        <li>
            Other feeding relationships may become more important.
        </li>

    </ul>

</section>


<!-- =========================================================
     STEP 11 — SYSTEMS THINKING

<section class="lesson">

    <h2>Step 9 — Systems Thinking</h2>

    <div class="question">

        Can a population change affect an organism
        that it does not directly interact with?

    </div>

    <p>

        Yes. Because organisms are interconnected,
        effects can move through multiple feeding relationships.

    </p>

</section>

<section class="international">

    <h2>
        Step 13 — Final International Summary
    </h2>

    <h3>
        Food Webs: Ecological Interdependence
    </h3>

    <p>

        A food web represents multiple interconnected feeding
        relationships within an ecosystem.

    </p>

    <p>

        Unlike a food chain, which shows one pathway,
        a food web represents the complexity of real ecosystems.

    </p>

    <div class="example">

        Fewer frogs → More insects →
        Greater pressure on plants

        <br><br>

        Fewer frogs → Less food for some predators →
        Possible predator decline

    </div>

    <div class="highlight">

        <strong>Key Message:</strong>

        <p>

            Ecosystems are interconnected systems.
            A change in one population can produce direct
            and indirect effects throughout the food web.

        </p>

    </div>
